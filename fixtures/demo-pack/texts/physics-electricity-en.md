# Chapter 12: Electricity

Notes on current, potential difference, resistance and the heating effect of current, with solved examples.

## 12.1 Electric Current and Charge

Electric charge is a basic property of matter, measured in coulomb (C). Charge always comes in whole-number multiples of the charge of one electron, e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, so Q = ne. One coulomb is the charge of about 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons.

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through any cross-section of a conductor: I = Q/t. The SI unit of current is the ampere (A). One ampere is one coulomb of charge flowing per second, so 1 A = 1 C/s.

- In a metal wire the moving charges are free electrons, which drift from the negative terminal towards the positive terminal.
- By convention, the direction of electric current is the direction of flow of positive charge, opposite to the flow of electrons.
- Current is measured with an ammeter, which has very low resistance and is always connected in series in the circuit.

Worked example: 0.5 A flows through a torch bulb for 10 minutes. Q = I × t = 0.5 × 600 = 300 C. Number of electrons n = Q/e = 300 ÷ (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 1.875 × 10²¹.

## 12.2 Potential Difference

Just as water flows from a higher level to a lower level, positive charge flows from higher potential to lower potential. A cell or battery maintains this potential difference across its terminals using the chemical energy stored in it.

The potential difference V between two points is the work done W to move a unit charge from one point to the other: V = W/Q. Its SI unit is the volt (V). The potential difference is 1 volt when 1 joule of work is done to move 1 coulomb of charge, so 1 V = 1 J/C.

- Potential difference is measured with a voltmeter, which has very high resistance and is always connected in parallel across the two points.
- A dry cell gives about 1.5 V; the household supply in India is about 220 V AC.

Worked example: 24 J of work moves 4 C across a resistor, so V = W/Q = 24 ÷ 4 = 6 V. Moving 3 C across 12 V needs W = VQ = 36 J.

## 12.3 Ohm's Law and Resistance

Ohm's law: the current through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends, provided its temperature and other physical conditions remain the same. So V ∝ I, or V = IR, where the constant R is the resistance of the conductor.

Resistance is the property of a conductor to oppose the flow of charge through it. Its SI unit is the ohm (Ω); 1 Ω = 1 V/A. The V–I graph of a metal wire is a straight line through the origin, and its slope gives the resistance. A rheostat is a variable resistor used to change the current in a circuit.

The resistance of a uniform wire depends on its length, area of cross-section, material and temperature. R ∝ L and R ∝ 1/A, so R = ρL/A, where ρ (rho) is the resistivity of the material. The SI unit of resistivity is the ohm metre (Ω m).

- Resistivity depends only on the material and its temperature, not on the length or thickness of the wire.
- Metals and alloys have very low resistivity (about 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁶ Ω m); insulators such as rubber and glass have about 10¹² to 10¹⁷ Ω m.
- Copper (about 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m) and aluminium are used for wiring and transmission lines.
- Nichrome, an alloy, has higher resistivity than its constituent metals and does not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperature, so it is used in heating elements of irons, toasters and heaters.
- When a wire is stretched to double its length, its area becomes half, so its resistance becomes four times.

Worked example: A bulb draws 0.5 A from a 220 V supply, so R = V/I = 220 ÷ 0.5 = 440 Ω. A copper wire 10 m long with cross-section 1 × 10⁻⁶ m² has R = ρL/A = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ × 10 ÷ 10⁻⁶ = 0.17 Ω.

## 12.4 Resistors in Series and Parallel

In series, resistors are joined end to end. The same current flows through each, and the total potential difference is V = V1 + V2 + V3. The equivalent resistance Rs = R1 + R2 + R3 is greater than the largest individual resistance.

In parallel, resistors are connected between the same two points. The potential difference across each is the same, and the total current is I = I1 + I2 + I3. The equivalent resistance is given by 1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3, and it is less than the smallest individual resistance.

- For n equal resistors of resistance R each, the equivalent resistance is nR in series and R/n in parallel.
- Household appliances are connected in parallel, so each gets the full supply voltage, can be switched on or off independently, and keeps working even if another fails.
- A series circuit has only one path, so if one bulb in a string of decorative lights fuses, all of them go off.

Worked example: Resistors of 3 Ω and 6 Ω are joined to an 18 V battery. In series, Rs = 9 Ω and I = 18 ÷ 9 = 2 A. In parallel, 1/Rp = 1/3 + 1/6 = 1/2, so Rp = 2 Ω and I = 18 ÷ 2 = 9 A: 6 A through 3 Ω and 3 A through 6 Ω.

## 12.5 Heating Effect of Current and Electric Power

When current flows through a resistor, part of the source's energy is converted into heat. Joule's law of heating: the heat produced is H = I²Rt, proportional to the square of the current, the resistance and the time. Using V = IR, H = VIt = V²t/R, measured in joule (J).

- Uses: electric iron, toaster, kettle, room heater and the filament bulb.
- A bulb filament is made of tungsten (melting point about 3400 °C); bulbs are filled with inactive nitrogen and argon to prolong filament life.
- A fuse is a wire of low melting point connected in series with the appliance; excess current melts it and breaks the circuit.

Electric power is the rate of consumption of electrical energy: P = VI = I²R = V²/R. The SI unit of power is the watt (W); 1 W = 1 V × 1 A. The commercial unit of electrical energy is the kilowatt hour (kWh), called one unit: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

Worked example: 2 A flows through a 10 Ω resistor for 5 minutes. H = I²Rt = 2² × 10 × 300 = 12000 J = 12 kJ.

Worked example: An iron is rated 1100 W, 220 V. I = P/V = 5 A and R = V²/P = 44 Ω. Used 2 hours daily for 30 days, energy = 1.1 kW × 60 h = 66 kWh; at 6 rupees per unit the bill is 396 rupees.
