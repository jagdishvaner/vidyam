## Judgement calls

- Every SSC list item maps to a node: semantic, number and figural analogy, classification and series; word building; coding-decoding; numerical and symbolic operations; trends; space orientation and visualization; Venn diagrams; drawing inferences; punched hole and pattern folding; figure completion; indexing; address and date/city matching; centre-code and roll-number classification; small and capital letter coding; embedded figures; critical thinking; emotional and social intelligence. SSC therefore links the whole subject, with weightage 0.25 (25/100).
- CSAT's "general mental ability" is broad and undefined. Based on recent papers, it links the verbal-reasoning chapters except clerical aptitude, non-verbal counting of figures and cubes and dice, and the logical, analytical and arithmetical units, plus the decision-making and interpersonal-skills chapters. Mirror and water images, paper folding, embedded figures, clerical aptitude and emotional and social intelligence are treated as SSC-only. CSAT states no section shares.
- CSAT's "Interpersonal skills including communication skills" is the chapter decision_making/interpersonal_skills. Its topics are relationships and teamwork, communication skills, conflict resolution, and workplace and public communication. The SSC whole-subject link covers this chapter too, because SSC's "Social Intelligence" and "decision making" test the same situational, interpersonal judgement. CSAT's "Comprehension" stays with the english tree.
- RPSC School Lecturer Paper I (rpsc_1g_paper_1) links exactly its five mental-ability items. The verbal analogy, series, coding-decoding and blood-relation chapters and the logical-Venn topic are linked directly. The figural-analogy and figural-series topics are also linked, because "analogy" and "series completion" are not limited to words or numbers. Venn-diagram counting is not included. No share is stated.
- Data sufficiency sits in quantitative_aptitude because UPSC lists it under data interpretation. Clocks and calendars sit here under arithmetical reasoning.
